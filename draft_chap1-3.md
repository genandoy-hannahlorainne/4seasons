CHAPTER I
The Problem and Its Setting
 Introduction and Background
Schools today play a major role in ensuring the safety and well-being of students. However, many clinics still use manual, paper-based medical records, causing delays, lost information, and difficulty retrieving health data during emergencies. To address this need, the researchers propose StudentCare+: A Centralized Digital Clinic Management System with QR Scanning for Students and SMS Alerts for Parents at PDMHS.
StudentCare+ modernizes the current workflow by digitizing medical records, integrating QR-based student retrieval, and enabling automated alerts for parents and advisers. The system improves clinic responsiveness, reduces paperwork, ensures secure storage, and enhances coordination between the clinic, parents, and school staff.

 Statement of the Problem
The general problem of this study is to determine how a centralized digital clinic management system can address the inefficiencies and challenges in managing student medical records at President Diosdado Macapagal High School (PDMHS). At present, the school relies on manual and paper-based documentation, which often results in delayed access to health information, communication gaps between the clinic and other stakeholders, and inconsistencies in recording student medical data. These issues affect the accuracy of medical records, the responsiveness of clinic staff, and the overall safety and well-being of students, especially during emergencies.
To address these concerns, the researchers propose StudentCare+: A Centralized Digital Clinic Management System with Role-Based Access and Automated Alerts for PDMHS. The system aims to modernize clinic operations by providing secure digital record management, improving communication among stakeholders, and ensuring timely dissemination of critical health information.
Specifically, this study seeks to answer the following questions:
1. What is the demographic profile of the respondents in terms of:
1.1 Position or role in the school (e.g., school nurse, adviser/teacher, parent, student)
1.2 Years of service or affiliation with PDMHS
2. What existing problems are encountered in the current clinic management process in terms of:
2.1 Medical record documentation and retrieval
2.2 Communication and information dissemination
2.3 Accuracy and consistency of health data
2.4 Response time during emergencies
3. How can StudentCare+ address these problems through:
3.1 Centralized and secure digital record management
3.2 Role-based access control that ensures data privacy and information security
3.3 Automated alerts and notifications for faster response and coordination
3.4 Improved communication among students, parents, advisers, and clinic staff
4. What is the level of effectiveness of StudentCare+ in terms of:
4.1 Accuracy of medical information encoded in the system
4.2 Speed, reliability, and timeliness of automated notifications
4.3 Ease of use and accessibility for different user roles
4.4 Reduction of workload and improvement in efficiency for clinic staff



Scope and Delimitations
Scope
The system will include:
Centralized medical record storage
Role-based access (Student, Clinic Staff, Adviser, Admin)
QR code student identification
SMS alerts for parents/advisers
Digital visit logs, vitals, immunizations
Automated report generation
Backup/restore module
Delimitations
No online appointment scheduling
No integration with hospitals or external EMR systems
No parent login account (SMS only)
Only text-based SMS alerts (no calls, no email templates)

Objectives of the Study
General Objective
To develop a centralized digital clinic management system that streamlines clinic operations and enhances student health monitoring.
Specific Objectives
Create a centralized database for secure and organized medical information.
Implement role-based access to ensure data privacy.
Develop QR scanning to quickly retrieve student records.
Implement an SMS alert system for parents during emergencies.
Design a user-friendly interface for students, advisers, and clinic staff.
Generate automated digital reports for school administration.
Implement backup and restore procedures to protect school medical data.

Students.
 The system allows students to benefit from faster access to their medical information, enabling more accurate health assessments and timely interventions. By having organized and updated health records, students receive improved medical support and safer, more efficient clinic services.
Parents/Guardians.
 The implementation of automated SMS alerts ensures that parents and guardians are immediately informed during medical emergencies or clinic visits. This enhances transparency, strengthens parental awareness, and promotes better coordination between the school and home.

Researchers/Developers.

 Definition of Terms
Automated Report Generation.
 A system feature that automatically produces summaries and reports based on stored medical data for administrative and clinic use.
Backup and Restore.
 A process of saving copies of digital records and recovering them when needed to prevent data loss.
Centralized Database.
 A unified storage location where all student medical records are organized, updated, and securely maintained.
Clinic Dashboard.
 A visual interface that displays real-time clinic information, such as student visits, alerts, and medical records.
Data Privacy.
 The protection of personal and sensitive medical information from unauthorized access or misuse.
Digital Clinic Management System.
 A computerized system that automates clinic processes such as recording, storing, and retrieving student medical information.
Electronic Medical Record (EMR).
 A digital version of a student’s medical history that includes health information such as vitals, consultations, and treatments.
Emergency Notification.
 An automated message sent during medical emergencies to inform parents or advisers immediately.
Health Information System (HIS).
 A digital platform used to collect, manage, and process health-related data within an organization.
QR Code (Quick Response Code).
 A machine-readable, two-dimensional barcode used to quickly access a student’s profile and medical record.
QR Scanning.
 The process of using a device to read QR codes for fast retrieval of student information.
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).
 A security method that grants system permissions based on the user’s role, ensuring data privacy and controlled access.
SMS Alert (Short Message Service Alert).
 An automated text message sent to parents or advisers to inform them about a student’s clinic visit or medical situation.
System Administrator (Admin).
 A person responsible for managing user accounts, system settings, backups, and overall system maintenance.
User Interface (UI).
 The visual and interactive elements through which users navigate and operate the system.
















CHAPTER II
Review of Related Literature Studies
This chapter presents relevant literature and previous studies that serve as the foundation of this research. These materials provide theoretical and empirical insights. The gathered literature and studies aim to support the rationale for the proposed system. 
Foreign Literature
According to Pandeeswari, Padmavathi, and Srilakshmi (2021) developed a QR-based paperless outpatient consultation and records sharing system aimed at improving the accessibility and security of patient health information. Their study addressed the common problem of managing outpatient medical histories, especially during periods of high patient volume such as the COVID-19 pandemic. The system stored consultation notes, prescriptions, and test results in QR codes, enabling fast retrieval and efficient sharing across clinics. This approach significantly reduced dependence on physical records and improved clinical workflow. The relevance of this study to the present research lies in its demonstration of how QR technology can streamline medical record retrieval, an approach similar to the “Digital Clinic Management System with QR Scanning” designed for students.

Kuo et al. (2022) introduced a smart health monitoring system using RFID and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies to automate and digitalize health data management in hospitals and public spaces. The system offered automated temperature monitoring, real-time patient data recording, and secure health information storage. It addressed challenges related to disease prevention, resource allocation, and rapid health data transmission. This literature supports the current study by emphasizing the importance of automated identification technologies in improving medical information accuracy and accessibility. Although the technology used differs from RFID instead of QR codes, the underlying principle of enhancing medical record efficiency remains consistent with the objectives of the digital clinic system.

According to Shah and Patel (2020) proposed a mobile-based electronic health record (EHR) system that allows patients and healthcare providers to access medical information conveniently through mobile devices. Their study emphasized the advantages of mobile platforms in terms of portability, real-time access, and secure storage of patient data. The system improved communication between doctors and patients, minimized data loss, and addressed issues related to traditional paper-based records. This literature is relevant to the current system because both aim to enhance digital access to patient records and ensure seamless interaction between users and healthcare workers, similar to the student-focused clinic management system.

Kumar and Dewan (2019) developed an SMS-based notification system designed to remind patients of upcoming appointments and medication schedules. Their study revealed that SMS alerts significantly reduced missed appointments and improved adherence to treatment plans. By providing timely communication, the system enhanced patient engagement and supported better clinical outcomes. This literature directly aligns with the proposed digital clinic system’s use of SMS alerts to notify students about medical updates, appointments, and health advisories, demonstrating the effectiveness of mobile messaging in healthcare communication

The study by Li and Wang (2018) explored the development of a digital school health information management system aimed at improving health monitoring and documentation among students. Their system centralized medical records, vaccination history, and health assessments, allowing school nurses and administrators to access up-to-date student information efficiently. The study found that digital databases reduced administrative workload and improved the accuracy of student health records. This literature directly supports the present study, as both systems concentrate on improving clinic operations within an educational institution and ensuring accurate student health information management.


Foreign Studies
Several international studies have contributed valuable insights into the digitalization of health-record systems, offering relevant perspectives for the development of a Student Medical Record System. Ali (2020) emphasized the increasing importance of Health Monitoring and Management Systems (HMMS) in supporting student wellness, particularly through early detection and continuous monitoring of health conditions. The study proposed a scalable HMMS for primary school learners that integrates health tracking, nutrient analysis, and data-driven alerts. These features highlight the role of digital tools in promoting proactive health management and fostering long-term well-being among students.
Similarly, Dikra Shareen (2024) addressed the persistent absence of modern health management systems in many educational institutions, which often results in poor documentation and delayed health interventions. The School Health Care Management System developed in the study digitalized essential medical processes, improving the accuracy and security of health records. The system also enhanced accessibility and strengthened coordination between school personnel, demonstrating the operational benefits of adopting digital health solutions in school settings.
Kobayashi et al. (2024) explored the potential of interoperable digital health technologies by introducing Sync for Science-J (S4S-J), a system designed to enable secure, controlled sharing of electronic medical records. Using QR codes and privacy-focused mechanisms, the system allows patients to manage how their data is accessed while ensuring compliance with global standards such as HL7 FHIR and LOINC. Their findings emphasize the importance of data portability, user privacy, and trust—key considerations in designing school-based digital medical record systems.
In another study, Innocon et al. (2023) developed an online medical record system equipped with SMS and email notification features to facilitate communication between patients and healthcare providers. Built using Object-Oriented Analysis and Design Methods (OOADM) and contemporary web technologies, the system improved data accessibility, record accuracy, and patient engagement. These results illustrate how automated communication tools can enhance responsiveness and support better healthcare workflows.
Furthermore, Sari et al. (2020) examined the design and implementation of hospital medical record systems utilizing both web and mobile platforms. The study highlighted the importance of structured system development, incorporating clear database designs, flowcharts, and user interfaces. Their findings showed that such systems improve registration processes, increase service speed, streamline record processing, and strengthen patient tracking. These design principles offer practical guidance for developing effective health-record systems, including those intended for school clinics.
Collectively, these foreign studies reinforce the growing global shift toward digital health solutions. They demonstrate that well-structured medical record systems—whether in schools, hospitals, or community-based settings—enhance efficiency, data security, and user engagement. Their insights serve as valuable references for designing a comprehensive, secure, and user-friendly Student Medical Record System.

Local Studies
Several local studies in the Philippines have explored the development and implementation of digital medical and health-record systems, providing a strong foundation for the creation of a Student Medical Record System. Bergado et al. (2020) developed the Computerized Medical Record and Monitoring System for Saint Michael College of Caraga (SMCC), which demonstrated that digital storage of student health information significantly improved the speed, security, and accuracy of clinic operations. Their system enabled faster retrieval of records and minimized the risks. associated with paper-based files, such as loss, damage, or duplication. This study highlights the practical advantages of digitalizing school clinic records in a Philippine educational institution.
Similarly, a project conducted at Bestlink College of the Philippines introduced a Clinic Management System integrating MySQL and C# technologies to record student consultations, illnesses, and treatments (USGBC IJMRA, 2021). The researchers reported that the system streamlined the clinic's record-keeping processes by centralizing student medical histories and reducing manual errors associated with handwritten logbooks. Their findings reinforce the value of computerized documentation in school health services, especially for institutions handling large student populations.
Another relevant local initiative is the Medical Record Management System of Baung Health Center in the Philippines. Although implemented in a community health-center setting rather than a school, the system proved effective in improving accuracy, accessibility, and data security through systematic electronic record-keeping (CSU Journal of Public and Allied Sciences, 2019). The study revealed how transitioning from manual to digital systems not only protects sensitive patient information but also improves workflow efficiency-insights that are directly applicable to school-clinic environments.
A broader national-level study by Elepaño et al. (2025) examined the implementation of electronic health records across multiple Philippine primary-care settings, including urban, rural, and remote locations. Their mixed-methods pilot study found that user acceptability of electronic health records was high when the system was simple and open-source, but acceptability decreased when replaced with a more complex and resource-intensive system. These findings emphasize the importance of designing school medical record systems that match the available infrastructure, training, and technical capabilities of school clinic staff.
Finally, the University of the Philippines Manila previously explored the development of a health information system intended to centralize and digitalize student medical data. The study demonstrated improved efficiency in record retrieval and reduced duplication of data entries, highlighting the effectiveness of digital solutions in school-based health management processes. The researchers noted that automated systems can enhance clinic responsiveness and support more accurate monitoring of student health conditions (UP Manila HIS Project, 2019).
Together, these local studies demonstrate a consistent trend toward digitalization in health record management in the Philippines. They provide clear evidence that computerized medical record systems-whether in schools, clinics, or primary-care facilities-lead to greater efficiency, accuracy, and security compared with manual systems. Their findings strongly support the need for a Student Medical Record System, offering practical insights into design considerations, user acceptability, data protection, and system sustainability within the Philippine context.

Synthesis of Literature
Overall, both local and foreign studies demonstrate that transitioning from manual, paper-based medical record systems to digital platforms significantly improves the accuracy, accessibility, data security, and operational efficiency of health-information management. Local studies in the Philippines show improvements in record retrieval, data protection, and workflow efficiency after adopting computerized systems in school clinics and community health centers (Bergado et al., 2020; CSU Journal of Public and Allied Sciences, 2019). Similarly, foreign studies emphasize enhanced data accuracy, better communication, and more secure information exchange through digital health technologies, including mobile platforms, interoperable systems, and automated monitoring tools (Ali, 2020; Kobayashi et al., 2024; Shah & Patel, 2020). 
Despite these advancements, the reviewed literature reveals several limitations. Many existing systems both local and international do not incorporate QR-based record retrieval, which enables fast access to student profiles during emergencies. Likewise, although some studies highlight the benefits of automated communication, very few integrate SMS alerts specifically designed for parents or guardians in a school setting. Additionally, earlier systems seldom apply role-based access control, resulting in potential privacy risks when managing sensitive student health data. 
Most importantly, there is a lack of research focusing on the unique operational needs of public high schools, where large student populations, limited resources, and delayed communication often hinder efficient clinic operations.

Research Gap
Based on the studies reviewed, most existing systems focus only on digitizing medical records, and many of them are designed for hospitals or community health centers. None of the studies fully address the specific needs of a public high school clinic like PDMHS. The literature shows improvements in recordkeeping, but there are still important areas that were not covered.
First, no study combines QR code scanning with school clinic medical records, which would allow faster access to student information during emergencies. Second, although some systems use notifications, none of the local studies include an automated SMS alert feature for parents, which is important for school safety and communication. Third, most systems do not have role-based access designed for different school users such as clinic staff, advisers, students, and administrators. Lastly, no study provides an automated backup and restore process suited for schools that still rely heavily on manual documentation and have limited technical resources.
Because of these gaps, there is still no complete system that supports fast student identification, real-time communication with parents, secure access for different roles, and reliable data protection for school clinics. This is why StudentCare+ is needed to fill these missing features and provide a system specifically designed for the daily operations of PDMHS.



CHAPTER III
Methodology
       	This chapter outlines the methodology utilized in the conduct of the study. It includes the research design, sources of data, research instruments, data-gathering procedures, and the statistical methods applied to analyze the information collected. This section establishes a structured framework that directs the systematic process of investigation and supports the reliability and validity of the study’s results.
 Research Design
This study employed a Descriptive Developmental Research Design to systematically investigate, design, and develop StudentCare+: A Centralized Digital Clinic Management System with QR Scanning for Students and SMS Alerts for Parents at PDMHS. The descriptive aspect of the study allowed the researchers to examine the existing manual processes of the school clinic, identify challenges in medical record management, and gather relevant information from stakeholders. 
Research Setting
The research will be conducted at President Diosdado Macapagal High School, focusing on the school clinic, medical personnel, advisers, and students.
 
Sources of Data
The study utilized both primary and secondary sources of data to ensure comprehensive information for the development and evaluation of StudentCare+: A Centralized Digital Clinic Management System with QR Scanning for Students and SMS Alerts for Parents at PDMHS.

Primary Sources
The primary data were collected directly from stakeholders who are actively involved in the daily operations of the school clinic and student health management. These include:
Clinic Staff – Nurses and medical personnel who handle student health records and consultations.
Advisers/Teachers – Faculty members responsible for monitoring student well-being during school hours.
Students – End-users of the clinic services whose medical records will be digitized.
Parents/Guardians – Recipients of SMS alerts regarding their child’s health status or clinic visits
Data from primary sources were gathered through interviews, surveys, and direct observation of current clinic processes.
Secondary Sources
Secondary data were obtained from existing literature, including:
Academic journals, articles, and research studies on health information systems, digital medical record management, and school clinic operations.
Guidelines and reports from the Department of Health (DOH) relevant to school health services.
Online databases and previous studies on electronic medical record systems, QR code applications, and SMS alert systems in educational settings.

Target Population
The study focused on individuals and groups who directly interact with the school clinic and its medical records. To align with the objectives of StudentCare+, the students of President Diosdado Macapagal High School (PDMHS) are identified as the primary target population, as they are the main beneficiaries of the digital clinic management system.
Students – Primary beneficiaries of the system, whose medical records, clinic visits, and health notifications are managed and monitored through StudentCare+.
Clinic Staff – Secondary users responsible for operating the system, recording student consultations, updating medical information, and managing clinic workflows.
Advisers/Teachers – Stakeholders who require access to timely health notifications to ensure student safety and proper classroom monitoring.
Parents/Guardians – Recipients of SMS alerts regarding student health updates and clinic visits, supporting better communication between home and school.
School Administrators – Oversight personnel responsible for evaluating medical data trends, ensuring compliance, and managing overall clinic reporting.
By gathering insights from these groups, the study was able to obtain a comprehensive understanding of current clinic operations, user needs, and technical requirements necessary for the successful development and implementation of the proposed digital system.
Primary Target Population
The primary target population includes the clinic staff of President Diosdado Macapagal High School (PDMHS), as they are responsible for managing student medical records, consultations, and health monitoring. Their direct involvement in daily clinic operations makes their experience essential in identifying system requirements and ensuring that the proposed digital clinic management system effectively improves workflow and record-handling efficiency.
Research Instruments
The study utilized several research instruments to gather comprehensive data from different user groups. A structured interview guide was prepared for clinic staff to capture insights on current clinic operations and system workflows. Survey questionnaires were administered to parents, advisers, and students to assess their experiences, expectations, and satisfaction with clinic processes and the proposed digital system. Additionally, a system prototype, developed through Figma alongside the actual website, was used to evaluate usability, interface design, and system functionality.
Instrument Validation
To ensure the reliability and validity of the research instruments, the survey questionnaires for parents, advisers, and students—as well as the interview guide for clinic staff—were reviewed by qualified experts. These included an IT Instructor, a Registered Nurse from the school clinic, and the Research Adviser. Their evaluations focused on clarity, relevance, and alignment with the study’s objectives. A pilot test was also conducted with a small group of respondents to identify unclear items and confirm that the instruments accurately measure user experiences, system needs, and workflow-related concerns.
Quantitative Instrument Validation
The survey questionnaire was validated using quantitative methods to ensure its accuracy and reliability. A pilot test was administered to a small group of clinic staff, teachers, and students who were not part of the main study population. The initial responses were analyzed using Cronbach’s Alpha to measure the internal consistency of the survey items.
Items that demonstrated low reliability or unclear wording were revised, refined, or removed to ensure that the instrument accurately measures the usability, efficiency, accuracy, and overall acceptability of the StudentCare+ Digital Clinic Management System. This validation process ensured that the final questionnaire provides consistent and dependable data for evaluating the proposed system.


Sampling Design
This study employed Purposive Sampling, a non-probability sampling technique used to intentionally select participants who have direct involvement with school clinic operations and digital record management. The respondents consisted of clinic staff who serve as the primary users of the system, advisers and teachers who monitor student well-being, students who regularly access clinic services, and parents who receive SMS alerts regarding their children’s health updates. 
This sampling method ensured that the individuals included in the study possessed the appropriate experience, familiarity, and knowledge necessary to provide meaningful insights that would contribute to the development, testing, and evaluation of the proposed system.
Ethical Consideration 
This study adhered to strict ethical guidelines to ensure the protection, rights, and welfare of all participants involved. Participation was entirely voluntary, and respondents were clearly informed about the purpose, scope, and objectives of the research before providing their consent. Confidentiality and privacy were fully maintained, ensuring that all collected information was used solely for academic purposes and handled with utmost security.
Participants were assured that their identities would remain anonymous and that no personal or sensitive details would be disclosed in any part of the study. They were also given the freedom to decline or withdraw from participation at any point without facing any negative consequences. Additionally, the development and handling of digital data were conducted in compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173), ensuring responsible and ethical management of all information throughout the study.


Development Tools
The development of StudentCare+: A Centralized Digital Clinic Management System utilized a set of modern software tools and technologies to ensure efficient development, scalability, and system reliability. Angular was used for building a dynamic and responsive front-end interface, providing a structured framework for creating user-friendly pages. Bootstrap supported the design process by offering responsive layouts and ready-made UI components to enhance usability.
For server-side development, PHP served as the primary backend language responsible for processing requests, handling data transactions, and managing communication between the client and the database. The system environment and deployment processes were containerized using Docker, ensuring that the system runs consistently across different machines and improving portability during development and testing.
To enable real-time SMS alerts for parents, the system integrated Twilio or Globe SMS API, which facilitated automated message sending for clinic notifications, appointment updates, and health-related alerts. These tools collectively contributed to the development of a secure, scalable, and efficient digital clinic management system.

System Boundaries
It defines what is included in the StudentCare+ Clinic Management System and what lies outside its operational control. These boundaries clarify the limits of the system’s functionality, the actors involved, and the processes it directly manages.
Inside the System (IN SCOPE)
These are the components, functions, and interactions that StudentCare+ fully manages or controls:
1. Digital Clinic Management
Creation, storage, updating, and retrieval of student medical record
Management of clinic visit logs, vitals, immunizations, and treatment records
Recording of student health incidents and generating medical history reports
2. QR-Based Student Identification
QR code generation for each student
Scanning of QR codes to retrieve student profiles and medical data instantly
3. SMS Notification Module
Automated sending of SMS alerts to parents/guardians during clinic visits or emergencies
Sending health-related updates to advisers
4. Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)
Student module (view limited profile/vaccination info, if applicable)
Clinic staff module (full medical encoding and clinic management)
Adviser module (view notifications and student health advisories)


Admin module (system settings, backups, user accounts)
5. System Administration
User account management (Admin)
Data backup and restore
System activity logs and audit trails
6. Reporting and Data Summaries
Automated report generation for school administrators
Summary of clinic visits, common illnesses, and health metrics
Outside the System (OUT OF SCOPE)
These are functions not included, not automated, or beyond StudentCare+ control:
1. External Medical Institutions
No linkage to hospitals, clinics, or government EMR systems
No DOH database or third-party interoperability
2. Parent Portal
Parents cannot log in to the system
Parents receive SMS only, no mobile app or email alerts
3. Appointment and Scheduling
No appointment reservation system for clinic visits
No scheduling of medical checkups or vaccinations within the system
4. Non-SMS Communication
No email notifications
No phone call integration
No multimedia (image/audio) communication
5. External Hardware Integration
No integration with medical devices like thermometers, BP monitors, etc.
QR scanning limited to standard mobile/desktop QR scanners
6. Offline/Remote Accessibility
System is for school-based use only
No remote access for parents or students outside PDMHS
7. Advanced Analytics
No predictive analytics, machine learning, or illness forecasting

Use Case Report
1. USE CASE SUMMARY
Use Case Title: UC–01: Student Medical Record Retrieval and Clinic Visit Processing
Brief Description:
This use case describes the process by which clinic staff retrieves a student’s medical record using QR scanning, records clinic visits digitally, updates health information, and automatically notifies parents and advisers via SMS.
Primary Actor:
Clinic Staff (School Nurse)
Secondary Actors:
Student
Parent/Guardian (SMS recipient)
Adviser/Teacher (SMS recipient)
System Administrator (for system maintenance)
2. PRECONDITIONS
Before the use case starts, the following must be true:
The student is enrolled at PDMHS and has a generated QR code.
The clinic staff is logged into StudentCare+ with valid role-based access.
The system is connected to the central database.
SMS gateway/API is active for sending alerts.
3. TRIGGER
The use case begins when:
A student arrives at the school clinic for consultation, OR
Clinic staff scans the student’s QR code to access medical records.

5. ALTERNATE FLOWS
A1 – QR Code Not Recognized
Student code is damaged or unreadable.
The system displays an error.
Clinic staff manually searches student name/student number.
Continues to Step 4 of the main flow.
A2 – System Cannot Connect to Database
System displays “Database Connection Failed.”
Clinic staff temporarily record visit on manual log.
Information is encoded into StudentCare+ once the system reconnects.
A3 – SMS Gateway Is Offline
The system saves clinic visits but cannot send SMS.
System logs the failed alert in the Activity Log.
Once the SMS gateway is restored, the system resends pending alerts.
6. EXCEPTION FLOWS
E1 – Unauthorized Access Attempt
The user attempts to access the clinic module without permissions.
The system restricts access and logs a security alert.
E2 – Incomplete Data Entry
Clinic staff omit required fields (e.g., vitals).
The system displays validation errors and does not proceed until completed.
7. POSTCONDITIONS
Successful Outcome
A student's medical visit is saved in the centralized database.
Accurate and updated medical record is stored.
SMS alert is delivered to parent/guardian.
The adviser receives timely notification.
Clinic dashboard updates in real time.
Failed Outcome
If the system is unavailable, no digital record is created.
SMS may fail if the network is down (logged for later sending).
8. BUSINESS RULES
RBAC Rule: Only clinic staff can encode or update medical records.
Privacy Rule: Students cannot view sensitive medical data.
SMS Rule: All clinic visits automatically trigger an SMS alert.
Backup Rule: System backups occur daily at 2:00 AM and during manual admin trigger.
9. SPECIAL REQUIREMENTS (NON-FUNCTIONAL)
Performance
QR code scanning must retrieve student data within 3 seconds.
SMS alerts must be sent within 30 seconds of data submission.
Security
All data stored in encrypted format.
Strict RBAC enforced between Students, Staff, Advisers, and Admin.
Usability
Designed with a clean, intuitive interface for non-technical clinic staff.
Reliability
Must operate even with unstable internet by using local caching (if part of the design).
10. EXTENSIONS / VARIATION POINTS
V1 – Emergency Case Handling
System triggers an Emergency SMS containing:
▸ Student full name
▸ Condition summary
▸ Time of emergency
▸ Request for immediate parent response
V2 – Adviser Health Summary View
Advisers can review limited medical advisories for students under their class.

Proposed System Architecture
1. Presentation Layer (Client Interface)
This layer consists of all user-facing components that allow interaction with the system
ComponentsWeb Application (Angular Frontend): Provides responsive and user-friendly interfaces for Students, Clinic Staff, Advisers, and Administrators.
QR Scanner Interface: Enables scanning of student QR codes using a device camera.
Browser-based Access (Mobile & Desktop): Supports cross-device usage within school facilities.
Responsibilities
Display dashboards and forms
Capture QR scans
Send user actions to the API
Show notifications, results, and visit logs
2. Application Layer (Backend / Server Logic)
This layer processes all logic, transactions, and system workflows.
Components
REST API (PHP / Laravel or PHP-based Framework): Handles all business logic and communication between frontend and database.
Authentication & Authorization (RBAC): Manages roles such as Admin, Clinic Staff, Adviser, and Student.

Visit Recording Module: Creates and updates medical visit logs.
QR Processing Module: Generates, encrypts, and validates QR codes.
SMS Notification Module: Sends automated SMS alerts to parents or advisers.
Reporting & Audit Log Module: Tracks activities and generates summaries.
Responsibilities
Validate inputs
Process clinical records
Trigger SMS notifications
Manage role permissions
Log user activity
3. Integration Layer (External Services)
This layer communicates with external APIs or services integrated into the system.
Components
SMS Gateway (e.g., Twilio, Globe): Sends parent/adviser notifications for clinic visits.
QR Code Generator & Reader Libraries: For producing unique student QR codes and scanning them.
Backup Service (Local + Cloud): Automated backup schedules and storage integration.
Responsibilities
Deliver SMS alerts
Manage QR code encryption
Support backup and restore operations

4. Data Layer (Database & Storage)
Handles all persistent data storage.
Components
Relational Database (MySQL/MariaDB): Stores medical records, users, QR identifiers, visit logs, and audit trails.
Backup Storage: Local server directory + cloud storage (optional) for redundancy.
Responsibilities
Store and secure all records
Handle queries from backend API
Maintain data integrity and relationships
Support point-in-time recovery

Quality Plan
1. Quality Standards
1.1 Functional Quality
All core features must meet the defined system requirements: QR scanning, centralized medical records, SMS notifications, user management, reporting, and backup/restore.
System modules must operate accurately and consistently with no loss of data integrity.
1.2 Security & Privacy Standards
Follows the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) principles: confidentiality, integrity, and proper handling of sensitive health information.
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) implemented to restrict access to authorized users only.
Use of encrypted connections (HTTPS) and secure storage for sensitive data.
1.3 Usability Standards
User interface must be intuitive, responsive, and easy to navigate for clinic staff, advisers, admin and students.
Workflows must minimize steps and reduce documentation time.
Compatible with desktop and mobile browsers.
1.4 Performance Standards
API response time must remain within acceptable limits (average < 300 ms).
QR scan and student record retrieval must occur within 1–2 seconds.
SMS notifications must be sent within 1 minute after record creation.
1.5 Reliability Standards
Daily automated backups must run without failures.
System uptime target: 99% availability.
No critical errors during transactions (e.g., record saving, QR reading).
2. Quality Assurance Activities
2.1 Requirements Validation
Maintain a requirement-to-test-case matrix to ensure each requirement is implemented and verified.
Conduct review sessions with the clinic staff and adviser representatives.
2.2 Code Review & Development Practices
All code commits undergo peer review to ensure standards, maintainability, and security.
Backend modules tested in isolated environments using developer test stubs.

2.3 Testing Procedures
Unit Testing: For QR module, SMS sender, validation rules, and user roles.
Integration Testing: Ensures coordinated operation between frontend, backend, and SMS gateway.
User Acceptance Testing (UAT): Conducted with actual clinic personnel during pilot deployment.
Usability Testing: Determine ease-of-use and identify workflow improvements.
2.4 Documentation & Prototype Validation
Review of UI mockups before implementation.
Early-stage prototype walkthroughs with stakeholders.
Validation of form structures and content before full development.
3. Quality Control Measures
3.1 Functional Testing Results
Measure the number of passed vs. failed test cases.
Target: 95–100% pass rate before deployment.
3.2 Data Integrity Checks
Validate entries for completeness and accuracy before saving.
Perform random spot-check audits of stored medical records.
3.3 Performance Monitoring
Track system load, API response times, and QR scanning speed.
Benchmark SMS delivery success rate (target ≥ 98%).

3.4 Error & Incident Tracking
Log all system errors, crashes, and exceptions.
Prioritize fixes based on severity and occurrence frequency.
3.5 Backup & Restore Verification
Monthly restoration drills to confirm backup reliability.
Verify data completeness after each restore.
1. Evaluation Methods
1.1 Survey Questionnaires
Conducted among clinic staff, advisers, and selected students.
Use Likert-scale instruments to measure usability, accuracy, speed, and system satisfaction.
1.2 System Performance Monitoring
Evaluate real-time logs: response time, SMS delivery status, number of visits recorded.
Measure system errors and downtime.
1.3 Usability Testing Sessions
Observe users performing tasks: scanning QR, adding records, sending notifications.
Record task completion time, number of clicks, and encountered difficulties.
1.4 Pre- and Post-Implementation Comparison
Compare manual clinic documentation vs. digital system metrics:
Time to retrieve student record
Time to notify parents
Error rates in documentation
1.5 Interview / Focus Group Discussion
Collect qualitative feedback to understand user pain points and suggestions.
2. Evaluation Criteria
2.1 Effectiveness
Accuracy of displayed student data and stored clinic records.
Correctness of SMS alerts sent to parents and advisers.
2.2 Efficiency
Reduction of documentation time (target: at least 30% faster than manual process).
Faster record retrieval through QR scanning.
2.3 Usability
Overall satisfaction score of ≥ 4.0 out of 5 in user surveys.
Minimal errors encountered by staff during use.
2.4 Reliability
Consistent system performance with no major downtime.
Successful SMS.
Proper access control and secure handling of medical data.
Successful backup and restore with no data loss.
3. Data Analysis
3.1 Quantitative Analysis
Compute mean, median, and standard deviation for survey responses.
Calculate success rates for SMS delivery, record saving, and QR scans.
Evaluate pre-/post-performance differences using:
Paired t-test (if data is normally distributed)
Wilcoxon Signed Rank Test (if non-normal)
3.2 Qualitative Analysis
Thematic coding of interview feedback.
Identification of recurring issues or improvement opportunities.
Categorization into usability, performance, and workflow themes.
3.3 Log & Metrics Analysis
Generate time-series charts for:
Response times
Error frequency
Backup success logs

3.4 Decision Metrics
Determine if the system meets acceptance thresholds:
Usability ≥ 4.0
